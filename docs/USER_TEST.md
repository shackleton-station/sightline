# User test: can a non-specialist answer in seconds?

The statement says Quickmap, Moon Trek and JPL Horizons are hard for non-specialists to use for rapid scenario
comparison. This test checks whether we did better, with a stopwatch instead of an opinion.

**Status:** not run yet. It needs people: Jessica and two non-engineers. Nothing here has been sent to anyone.

**About 20 minutes per person.** Run it live or on a video call, and don't help.

## Setup

- Use a person who hasn't seen the app. Once they've seen it, they can't be a first-time user again.
- Half start with our app, half with the other tool, so learning from the first doesn't favor the second.
- Pick one other tool per person: [Quickmap](https://quickmap.lroc.asu.edu) or
  [Moon Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/moon/).
- Open each tool's home page. In ours, clear the link so it starts with no `#` part.

## Say this

> We're testing the tool, not you. Please think out loud. I can't answer questions, so if you're stuck, say so. You can
> give up on any task at any time, and that's useful to us too.

## The three tasks

Read each one aloud, start the stopwatch, and stop it when they say the answer.

| # | Task | Correct answer comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "At Shackleton Rim on 14 May 2027 at noon UTC, is there sunlight, and can you see Earth? How long until sunset?" | Answer card, at that date |
| 2 | "Your lander needs the Sun and Earth up on landing day. Your May 2027 date at Connecting Ridge just slipped a month. Is the new date still OK? If not, name another date or another site." | Slip panel, or the grid |
| 3 | "Across all the sites, which month in 2027 looks best at the south pole for having both Sun and Earth in view?" | Grid |

Work out the correct answers from the live app just before each session, in case the data has changed.

## Record

Use first names or initials only, and no personal details beyond that.

Cap each task at 5 minutes and record it as "gave up at 300 s".

## Afterward, ask

1. What was the most confusing moment?
2. What did you expect to be able to do that you couldn't?
3. What do you think the Learn and Plan buttons do? (Ask before explaining them.)
4. Would you show this to a student or a colleague? Why or why not?

## How to use the result

- **Only quote what actually happened.** With three people this is a small sample: say "three first-time users", never
  "users are 15 times faster".
- **The other tools aren't designed for these exact tasks,** and they do much more than we do. A fair claim is about
  these tasks, not about the tools overall.
- **Fix before quoting.** Anything that makes a person stop or guess wrong matters more than the timing. Fix it, and
  retest with someone new.
- **A wrong answer found quickly is a failure,** not a win. Check accuracy before speed.
- Write what you learned into `FRICTION_LOG.md` and the Saturday plan in `docs/LESSONS.md`.
